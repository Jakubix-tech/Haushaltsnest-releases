Privacy policy

Haushaltsnest · 26 October 2026

Deutsche Fassung · Delete your data

Haushaltsnest is an app for your own household: stock, shopping list, tasks, rules, wishes, rewards. This policy says which data comes about, where it is kept and how to get rid of it again.

The short version. Without signing in, not a single item of data leaves the phone. With an account, the household's data is kept on a server so that several devices see the same state – and nowhere else. There are no ads, no analytics, and nothing is passed on to third parties for their own purposes.

2. Without an account: nothing leaves the device

The app works fully without an account. In that case all data lives in a database on the phone itself. No connection to a server of this project is made and no data is transmitted – no metrics, no crash reports, no device identifiers.

The app's error messages are written to the device's system log only. They stay there and are not sent anywhere.

One exception that does not come from this app: if the user wants it, Android backs up installed apps' data to the user's own Google Drive account ("automatic backup"). That includes this app's database. It is the user's own backup into their own account – this app neither triggers it nor learns about it. It can be switched off in the Android settings under Google → Backup.

3. With an account: what is transmitted

Signing in exists for one reason: to share a household across several devices. For that, the following data is transmitted and stored:

From the Google account

  • the email address
  • the Google account identifier
  • the display name, if Google supplies one

Signing in goes through Google. No password is handed to this app and none is stored anywhere.

The household's content

  • the names of the profiles in the household – including children's, see section 5
  • products, quantities, storage places, shopping list, history of all entries
  • tasks with their assignment, completions and absences
  • rules, recorded breaches and their consequences
  • outings, wishes, votes cast
  • rewards with amounts and whether they have been paid out
  • photos of products and places, scaled down to about 512 pixels
  • invitation codes, for as long as they are valid

Purpose: keeping the household on one state across several devices. Without this transfer the feature would not exist.
Legal basis: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR – the processing is necessary for the feature that was asked for.

What is explicitly not transmitted

  • A profile's PIN. It protects against someone else on the same device ticking off tasks in another person's name. For that it does not need to go anywhere: it is stored as a PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hash on the device, and the column for it deliberately does not exist on the server.
  • Location, contacts, calendar, phone numbers. The app asks for none of these.
  • Usage statistics. No analytics or crash-reporting service is built in.

4. Who processes the data

One processor is used for running the server:

  • Supabase Inc., 970 Toa Payoh North, Singapore – database, file storage for the photos, and sign-in. The data is held in Ireland (region eu-west-1), that is, inside the European Union. Supabase Inc. is based outside the EU; in so far as the data is accessed from there – during maintenance, for instance – that happens on the basis of the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.
  • Google Ireland Limited – only for signing in with a Google account and for distributing the app through Google Play. Google learns that an account has signed in to this app; Google does not learn the household's content.

The app's reminders are scheduled and raised on the device itself. No push service is used, so there is no server that learns when somebody is reminded of something.

Nothing is passed on to any further third party. The data is not sold and not used for advertising.

5. Children in the household

The app is meant for households where children take part. A child does not get an account: an adult creates a profile that lives on the device and is shared through the household. What is processed is the child's first name or nickname, their tasks and completions, rules and consequences that concern them, and wishes and rewards.

Whoever creates the household decides about the children's data in it and is responsible for it. A first name is enough – entering full names, dates of birth or photographs of children is neither necessary nor intended.

The app contains no advertising, no in-app purchases and no links to third-party content that would be unsuitable for children.

6. Permissions and what they are for

  • Camera – reading barcodes and QR invitations, taking photos of products and places. Everything else stays usable without this permission.
  • Notifications – the daily reminder about open tasks and the messages about new rules, wishes and rewards.
  • Internet and network state – syncing with the server.
  • Run after boot – so that the recurring sync survives a restart of the device.

7. How long the data is kept

As long as the household exists. There is no automatic deletion after a set period, because a stock and a task history live precisely on being kept.

Deleted items usually disappear from every device within seconds, as soon as those devices sync next. Invitation codes expire by themselves after the period stated when they were issued.

How to delete data or the whole account is on its own page: Delete your data.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to

  • access to the stored data (Article 15),
  • rectification (Article 16),
  • erasure (Article 17),
  • restriction of processing (Article 18),
  • data portability (Article 20),
  • object (Article 21).

Two of these are built into the app itself, without having to ask anybody: under Profile → Backup → Export you get the complete state as a readable file, and under Profile → Delete account the account is removed with all its data.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection supervisory authority.

9. Changes

If something changes in the app that affects this policy, the policy is updated. The date at the top says which version applies.
